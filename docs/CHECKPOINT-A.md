# Checkpoint A — learning and grading

The course retains fourteen lesson IDs and all learning-item IDs. Current lessons have 9–12 core tasks (145 total). New letters have explicit teaching cards, recognition and recall; subsequent lessons include 2–3 older sounds. No matching-grid exercises remain.

English sound recall has three deterministic outcomes: exact standard label, explicitly approved sound approximation, or incorrect. Approvals are a/ah, i/ee, u/oo and initial g/k, d/t, b/p. No approximation is accepted for eo, eu, o, n, m, s or initial silent ㅇ. In particular e cannot pass for ㅣ, and ng cannot pass for initial ㅇ. These alternatives do not apply to syllable spelling, Hangul input or meaning questions. Partial success progresses normally, records understanding, receives lower evidence weight, grants no exact independent recall, and returns sooner to review spelling.

Wrong answers append a different-format correction near the end. A session has at most three distinct correction items; correction questions cannot add another retry. All original mistakes remain in history. Hints are shown only when requested, including on corrections.

Existing mastery counters, delays, dates and history remain. Optional outcome and evidence fields add confidence without reinterpreting old answers. Recognition and hints earn less evidence than exact independent recall; full mastery still requires varied exact recall after a delay. Learner labels are Learning, Practicing and Remembered, without accuracy formulas or counters.

The first Korean typing task offers the exact Gabe-help message, I'm ready and Skip for now. A deferral skips Korean typing for that session without grading or mastery writes, persists across refresh, and offers a fresh choice in a later session. English-letter questions never trigger that help.

The storage namespace and version remain unchanged. All released exercise IDs stay registered for unfinished sessions, including tasks shortened out of the new core sequence. Former matching IDs now resolve to ordinary recognition. Existing matching selections are translated to the chosen sound label while their original mapping is archived in the session; index, queue, completion and historical evidence remain. Long old choice labels are translated on load, without writing to raw storage merely by loading. Previously resolved feedback/history is retained.

Checkpoint A checks include targeted unit tests, the curriculum validator, TypeScript, production build, and an automated full-course browser journey covering partial-credit feedback, ready/skip decisions, corrections, resume and progress. Physical iPhone audio/native-speaker review are not claimed.

Checkpoint B remains the separate finishing stage: quiet local-day streak, final iPhone layout work, audio checks, lighter family-phrase selection, final integration verification and production publication.
